Sticks and stones

Naming things matters

Core Narrative: Traditional SEO is gone

Direct answers on results pages via AI summaries AIO, cause over half of all searches to end without a click. Google has shifted from a doorway into a final destination. And if you aren't in the LLM, you are invisible.

Key Point: AI Procurement is Coming

In addition to LLMs, AI Agents are now looking at procurement and if they can’t understand or misconstrue your data, you will stop turning up.

The Paradigm Shift

From Rich Snippets to Machine Maps

Core Narrative: Traditional to AI-Readable

Google Schema used to be about ranking top in the list of blue links (SERPS). Today, it’s a layer of translation between human-focussed web content and AI data engines.

Key Point: Structured Entities over Strings

Generative search engines (Various LLMs, AI Overviews) don't just index text strings, they synthesise structured entities and measure relationships.

"Inference is much less reliable than instruction."

Relying on LLMs to guess what our software or organisation is by expecting them to read human-focussed marketing content can lead to missed citations and a loss of control over our brand narrative.

📌 Source: Aggarwal et al. (Princeton / Georgia Tech) – GEO: Generative Engine Optimisation (arXiv:2311.09735)
The Core Problem

Confusion Can Cause Chaos

Core Narrative: Our Biggest Blind Spot

Internal inconsistency is our biggest blind spot in the generative age. Relying on contradictory or misaligned labels weakens entity clarity.

Key Point: Mixed Signals to Crawlers

When marketing labels a tool a ‘Product’, documentation calls it an ‘App’, sales copy uses metaphors, and pages lack consistent structured data, machine crawlers get mixed signals. For example, until August 2026 Google Schema for Mosaic on the Kolekti site was ‘Product’, on Atlassian’s MPAC Mosaic it was listed as ‘Software App’ and on Docs there still appears to be no schema at all.

The Reality: Degrading Entity Confidence

By confusing the description of any entity we force search engines and LLMs to decode contradictory descriptions, read on-page marketing copy and Docs—all of which confuse the AI and erode their confidence.

"Structured data is defined as a standardized format for providing explicit information about a page's content to search engines and AI platforms." — Space and Story
📌 Source: Space & Story – The Complete Guide to Structured Data for AI Search
The Strategy

Beyond App Schema: Build a Coherent Knowledge Graph

Core Narrative: Beyond App Markup

Some initial studies suggest basic schema doesn't directly move traditional rankings, and that's fine, because we aren't stopping at standalone app markup.

Key Point: AI Alignment

Structuring explicit entity relationships ensures that whether an LLM encounters our product via sales copy, docs, or third-party mentions, all nodes map back to a single source of truth. So we use Schema as the bedrock and then translate that into all other messaging.

Strategic Takeaway: Interconnected Graph

We need to build an interconnected, site-wide Knowledge Graph to link our key corporate entities:

Organisation ➔ Author ➔ SoftwareApplication ➔ AppFeatures

This starts with the correct labelling of who we are, what we are offering and to whom.

"Developing a universal semantic layer is now a must‑do for D&A leaders either leading or supporting AI. It is the only way to improve accuracy, manage costs, substantially cut AI debt, align multiagent systems, and stop costly inconsistencies before they spread." — Gartner
📌 Source: Gartner Predictions: Semantic Layers
Governance & Standardisation

Schema as an Operational Framework

Core Narrative: Team Alignment

Schema isn't just a technical SEO trick for developers; it’s an operational standard for team content alignment.

Key Point: Upfront Guardrails

Agreeing on naming, taxonomy, and entity labeling upfront sets clear guardrails across ALL content, product, and engineering teams. As Schema is the most stripped down, it makes sense to start there.

Strategic Takeaway: Operational Harmony

Standardised schema ensures page content, metadata, and site architecture work in harmony, preventing future content drift and intent overlap before they ever hit production.

"Understanding how Google links entities in the Knowledge Graph can help to ensure that the entities and topics you want are connected to your brand. It can also uncover connections between entities you don’t want associated with your brand." — Search Engine Land
📌 Source: Search Engine Land – What Is the Knowledge Graph? How It Impacts SEO & Visibility
Summary & Action Plan

The Way Forward: Clarity, Organisation, Discovery

1

Standardise Vocabulary

Establish agreed naming conventions for core products and software across all team silos.

2

Implement Cross-Entity Schema

Deploy agreed schema for organisations, apps, authors, and any entity touchpoints. (The Sanity CMS has a ‘deploy once’ feature, if we don’t use that use a ‘Same As’ pointer.)

3

Enforce Single Canonical Paths

Protect entity authority and stop internal confusion before publishing.

Closing Thought

"We are no longer just putting up signposts for search crawlers, we are building the map that AI relies on to navigate the entire space."
