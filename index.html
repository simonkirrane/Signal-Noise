01 / THE LANDSCAPE

Naming things matters

Traditional SEO has radically changed

Direct answers on Google results pages (AIO), cause over half of all searches to end without a click. Google has shifted its model and if AI can't confidently understand what you are, what you do and what you sell, it won't surface you. On top of that, generative search engines naturally default to established category leaders: if a challenger or specialised product cannot unambiguously instruct the AI on its exact capabilities, pricing, and audience, you'll likely be ignored.

AI Procurement is Coming

In addition to LLMs, AI Agents are now moving to procurement and if they can’t understand or misconstrue your data, you will not show up.

02 / THE PARADIGM SHIFT

From Rich Results to Machine Maps

Traditional to AI-Readable

Google Schema used to be about ranking top in the list of blue links (SERPS). Today, it’s a layer of translation between human-focussed web content and AI data engines.

Structured Entities over Strings

Generative search engines (Various LLMs, AI Overviews) don't just index text strings, they synthesise structured entities and measure relationships.

Case in Point: How Brave Ask Actually Reads the Web

This isn't theoretical. DEJAN AI's reverse-engineering of Brave's Ask system instructions shows the mechanism in production. Brave's pipeline doesn't hand a language model whole pages to interpret — it breaks roughly 210 retrieved pages down into an average of 6,257 discrete statements before the model ever sees them.

The model is explicitly instructed to ground every claim in that extracted evidence and is barred from filling gaps with its own knowledge. Separately, Brave's LLM Context API confirms this discipline extends to the retrieval layer itself: when converting pages into structured context for LLMs, the system preserves JSON-LD schemas and table data with row-level granularity, rather than flattening everything into raw text. Structured markup on the page survives the journey into the model's context — it isn't discarded in favour of prose.

Humans like storytelling and that's what marketing copy does, it's nuanced and human. LLMs like entities and relationships. So if we force LLMs to guess what our software or organisation is by making them read human-focussed marketing content we can confuse them leading to poor or no results. With LLMs inference is much less reliable than instruction.

"The core mission remains unchanged: to connect your solution with the people looking for it. However, the mechanism has shifted from “keywords and links” to “entities and trust.” By structuring your data for agents, building radical authority through experience, and owning the “messy middle” of comparison, you can ensure that your brand isn’t just found—it is chosen."

03 / THE CORE PROBLEM

Confusion Causes Chaos

Our Biggest Blind Spot

Internal inconsistency is our biggest blind spot in the generative age. Relying on contradictory or misaligned labels weakens entity clarity.

Mixed Signals = Noise

When marketing labels a tool a ‘Product’, documentation calls it an ‘App’, sales copy uses complex metaphors, and pages lack consistent structured data, machine crawlers get mixed signals. For example when I audited our Schema, I found our app was classified as 'Product' on our site, 'Software App' on the Marketplace, and absent from our documentation. We fixed this by establishing a single canonical definition ensuring every touchpoint used consistent entity typing.

The Reality: Degrading Entity Confidence

By confusing the description of any entity we force search engines and LLMs to try to decode contradictory descriptions, read on page marketing copy and Docs, all of which confuse the AI and erode their confidence. It can also mean you don't show up in LLMs or procurement agents.

04 / THE STRATEGY

Beyond App Schema: Build a Coherent Knowledge Graph

The Semantic Shift

When every department creates different descriptions of the same thing. AI systems don't reconcile those differences as well as humans do. Therefore, organizations need one canonical semantic representation.

Schema is the medium, not the message

The narrative here is not "Let's implement structured data" it's "Let's create a canonical semantic model for the company." Schema is important, but not just as a technical checkbox at the bottom of the stack; it is the canonical semantic model for the company. Agree on the shared ontology first, then express it consistently through Schema and let everything else inherit from it. That way LLMs, customers, partners and procurement agents all understand who and what you are, what you're selling and what it does.

Structured Context Beats Model Size

Brave's own benchmarking makes the case concrete. Running on the smaller, open-weight Qwen3 model, their Ask Brave assistant which is grounded in the same structured context pipeline that preserves JSON-LD schema and table data (as described on slide 2, outperformed Google AI Mode and ChatGPT on a 1,500-query evaluation. A smaller model with better structured context beat larger ones. This is the direct payoff of the argument in Section 2: schema isn't a nice-to-have for AI visibility, it's a measurable input to answer quality that a bigger model can't fully compensate for.

Strategic Takeaway: Schema as the Bedrock of Messaging

Start with Schema to define your reality, then build all messaging upon it.

We use explicit Schema to anchor every dimension of our business for machines:

  • What it is: Defining the product as a SoftwareApplication with explicit featureList properties.
  • Who owns it: Attaching verified Organization and author credentials.
  • What it costs: Hardcoding clear pricing tiers and trial parameters (Offer).
  • Where else it lives: Using sameAs to connect external channels—social profiles, YouTube demos, and marketplace listings.

Once this machine-readable source of truth is locked, we translate it directly into our marketing copy, documentation, and external touchpoints. Agree on the Schema first, and let the content flow from there.

05 / GOVERNANCE & STANDARDISATION

Schema as an Operational Framework

Team Alignment

Schema isn't just a technical SEO trick for developers; it’s an operational standard for team content alignment.

Upfront Guardrails

Agreeing on naming, taxonomy, and entity labeling up front sets clear guardrails across ALL content, product, and engineering teams. As Schema is the most stripped down, it makes sense to start there.

Strategic Takeaway: Operational Harmony

Standardised Schema ensures page content, metadata, and site architecture work in harmony, preventing future content drift and intent overlap before they ever hit production.
